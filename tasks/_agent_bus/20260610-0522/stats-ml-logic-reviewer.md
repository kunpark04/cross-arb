## Verdict

**Partially sound — the method is honest, the numbers reproduce to the digit, the OOS split is genuinely causal and NOT materially leaked, but the headline +462% OOS result is a single-position artifact, not evidence of generalization.** I reproduced every figure exactly (TEST A $142.05/+7127%, $7.83/+298%; TEST B $5.15 / $28.93(+462%) / $4.99(-3%) / $8.30(+61%); TEST C FIFO→$0 at hc≥0.5c). The split is strictly time-causal (early.open_t max 1781020975 < late.open_t min 1781020979, disjoint) and `window_end` on the test run is computed over the late half only — no train info leaks into the fit. **The 21/103 markets that appear in both halves are re-detections, not leakage** — they contribute only $1.30 (5%) of the FITTED test PnL, and tau is a fixed scalar, not a per-market fit, so there is no memorization channel. The real problem is variance, not contamination: **$26.90 of the $28.93 FITTED OOS PnL (93%) is ONE econ contract** (`urc-us-seasonadj-gte-june-2026-07-02-atl4pt4`, 12.2c edge, depth 423). Re-cut the split at frac 0.6/0.7 and FITTED funds exactly that ONE position; the +462% inflates to +801%/+1237% only because the FIFO denominator shrinks. So the OOS test does not demonstrate that a *learned threshold generalizes* — it demonstrates that *one fat econ arb existed in the late window and a high tau found it*. The trustworthy claim is the **FIXED RULE (2c + 5% cap): +61%, 11 funded, top1=20% / top3=47% of PnL** — that one is diversified and is the deployable, non-oracle result. The script itself says exactly this in its caveats; the over-reach is only if someone quotes "+462% OOS" as the validation. Treat the whole thing as a **method demonstration on 0.86 d / 226 candidates**, not validation.

## Methodological Audit

- **Choice of test (causal temporal split + grid-fit-on-train / apply-fixed-to-test):** Correct and appropriate for an allocation-policy backtest. `split_by_open_t` (clip_threshold_test.py:41-49) sorts by `open_t`, cuts at the frac-quantile, and assigns `< cut` to train and `>= cut` to test — genuinely disjoint and time-ordered. `best_tau_clip` (52-60) grid-searches **on the train half only** and the returned (tau*,clip*) are applied to the late half **without re-tuning** (test_B_oos:118-127). This is the right shape for an overfitting test. Verified by selftest assertion (clip_threshold_test.py:215) and independently (early.max 1781020975 < late.min 1781020979).
- **Economics reuse is apples-to-apples:** `P()` → `run_policy` (alloc_policy_experiment.py:80) → per-contract math lifted verbatim from `account_sim.run_account` (cost_per = max(0.1,1-net); profit = size·max(0, (net+0.005)/2 − haircut − void)). The only degrees of freedom across arms are consideration order + tau gate + clip cap. Confirmed identical expressions at account_sim.py:59,68-70 vs alloc_policy_experiment.py:45-54.
- **Assumptions that hold:** (a) capital ~does not recycle in-window (settlement proxy = event-date+28h ≫ 0.86 d window), so FIFO vs reservation differ only by *which* arbs get funded, not by recycle timing — valid here; (b) one position per market (C8 de-double-count) verified working — the dominant econ market is 1 raw episode → 1 after `one_per_market`, no inflation.
- **Assumptions NOT met / untested:** (a) **n is far too small for any OOS claim** — 103 test candidates over 0.43 d, and the winning policy funds 3 (or 1). (b) **No friction in TEST B** — the OOS PnL is gross/paper; the same friction that zeroes FIFO in TEST C is not applied to the FITTED OOS number. (c) **Settlement is a slug-date proxy**; the econ position settles 2026-07-02 (3 weeks out) — its "edge" is entirely unrealized/locked, never marked against an actual print.
- **Alternatives not considered:** (i) **Per-position bootstrap / leave-one-market-out** on the test half — would immediately expose that one contract carries the result. (ii) **Report PnL with a CI**, not a point — there is no uncertainty quantification anywhere. (iii) **Block/cluster resample by event-date or category** — the candidates are not IID (same-city weather buckets, same-game sports legs are correlated). (iv) **K-fold over multiple non-overlapping windows** once weeks of data exist, instead of one 50/50 cut.

## Result Interpretation

- **Statistical significance:** None is computed, and on this n none would survive. The FITTED OOS "edge" is n_funded=3 with 93% in one position → effectively n=1. No CI, no bootstrap, no permutation null. A leave-one-out on the test half would drop FITTED from $28.93 to ~$2.02 by removing one contract.
- **Practical significance (domain units):** The deployable claim is the **FIXED RULE: +$3.15 over FIFO ($8.30 vs $5.15) on a $500 paper bankroll over ~0.43 d**, gross of execution friction, diversified across 11 positions (top1 20%). That is directionally sensible (threshold + modest cap beats naive FIFO) but economically tiny and pre-friction. The +462% FITTED number is not practically meaningful — it is one econ contract you would need to actually fill at 423 depth and hold 3 weeks.
- **Multiple-testing / fragility:** TEST A's tau* and clip* are **in-sample oracle** (the script labels them so). TEST B's grid is 8 taus × 6 clips = 48 cells fit on train; the winner (5c,1000) is a max over 48 — modest selection but the script correctly applies it fixed to test. The real fragility is the **split-point sensitivity**: across frac ∈ {0.3,0.4,0.5,0.6,0.7} the FITTED gain swings +462%→+1237% and n_funded 3→1, because the denominator (FIFO) and the single-arb numerator move independently. This is the signature of a result resting on one observation.
- **What the result licenses you to conclude:** (1) A global edge threshold beats FIFO-by-arrival **in this window** — robust direction, because FIFO's pathology (fund the first thin arb to bankroll exhaustion) is real and the threshold structurally avoids it. (2) The clip cap alone is a weak PnL lever (+298% in A, **−3%** in B) — its value is risk-control (exposure per pair), not return; confirmed by the tau=5c column where PnL rises monotonically 11.52→142.05 with clip 25→1000 at fixed funds=5, skip_cap=0 (capital is NOT binding after thresholding, so capping just leaves cash idle). **What it does NOT license:** any claim that the threshold *generalizes out of sample* in a statistically meaningful sense, or any specific magnitude (+462%, +7127%).

## Impact on Project Scope

- **If the result stands (as a method demo, which is its honest framing):** the clip-stage design should be **threshold-primary, cap-as-risk-control** — fund in arrival order but gate on per-contract edge ≥ tau, with a modest per-pair clip cap whose job is exposure limiting, not PnL. The FIXED RULE (2c + 5%-of-bankroll cap) is the sane default to carry forward. This is consistent with the project's existing finding that capital is locked (no recycle) so *selection* dominates *ordering*.
- **If the result is fragile (it is, on magnitude):** do NOT use any number from this test to size capital, set a tau in production, or claim an OOS-validated edge. The +462%/+7127% figures are not decision-grade. Premature downstream decisions to avoid: picking tau=5c (it's the in-sample oracle and the OOS winner only because of one econ arb), or concluding "the threshold is worth ~5x" — that multiple is one contract.

It is consistent with (a) the documented FIFO/capital-lock model in capital_sim/account_sim, (b) the "edge lives in inefficient corners, capacity in depth" thesis (the one fat econ arb is exactly a transient depth-and-edge co-occurrence), and (c) the project's own repeated insistence (<1 day → PRELIMINARY, paper/gross).
