Yes — on paper there are several cross-platform arbs, and the 90-91°F pair is the fat one.

The two books have swapped their most-likely bucket: Kalshi’s mode is 90-91°F (64%), Polymarket’s is 88-89°F (50%). That single disagreement lets you buy the cheap side of the same outcome on each venue and lock a guaranteed payout — if both legs settle off the identical reading.

1 The disagreement that creates it

Both venues price a mutually-exclusive, collectively-exhaustive set of 2°F buckets. Their implied distributions are coherent internally (each set of Yes-asks sums to ~107¢, i.e. normal vig) but they disagree sharply with each other on which bucket is most likely:

Kalshi says 90-91 is the favorite; Polymarket says 88-89. They essentially traded places — which is exactly what makes the 88-89 and 90-91 hedges profitable in opposite directions.

2 The mechanism

For any one bucket, “Yes” (it happens) and “No” (it doesn’t) are complementary. Buy Yes on the venue where Yes is cheaper and No on the venue where No is cheaper. Exactly one leg pays $1, so if the two prices add to under 100¢, the difference is locked profit:

It’s purely a cross-venue opportunity — neither book is beatable on its own (each set of Yes-asks already sums to >100¢).

3 Specific example — the 90-91°F pair

The biggest edge. Kalshi prices 90-91 at 64% but Polymarket at only 39%, so 90-91 “Yes” is cheap on Polymarket and 90-91 “No” is cheap on Kalshi. Buy both:

Mirror trade — 88-89°F

Here Kalshi is the cheap-Yes side. Buy YES 88-89 on Kalshi @ 31¢ + NO 88-89 on Polymarket @ 52¢ = 83¢ → guaranteed $1 → +17¢ (~+15¢ net).

Why both directions work

90-91 is overpriced on Kalshi / underpriced on Poly; 88-89 is the reverse. So you buy the underpriced “Yes” on whichever venue is low and the cheap “No” on the other — the swapped modes feed both hedges at once.

4 Why this is not free money

⚠ Risk #1 — Settlement-source mismatch (the killer)

It’s a true arbitrage only if both venues resolve off the same reading. Kalshi settles Miami on the official NWS daily high (station KMIA); Polymarket may use a different source/station/API. Buckets are only 2°F wide, so a 1°F source difference near a boundary flips the outcome — and then both legs can lose.

Example on the 90-91 pair: Kalshi reads 90 (→ in 90-91, your Kalshi-NO loses) while Polymarket reads 89 (→ your Poly-YES 90-91 loses). Result: −$0.76, not +$0.24. Kalshi’s own banner — “Not all weather data is the same” — is flagging exactly this.

Other frictions to clear first
  • 2.Same day? Kalshi says “today,” Polymarket says “June 8.” Confirm they’re the same calendar day in Miami’s timezone (ET) — otherwise they’re different events.
  • 3.Liquidity. Polymarket “Vol” on the two fat buckets is only ~$1.6-1.8k, and that’s cumulative volume, not book depth. The quoted price likely fills only small size before the edge collapses.
  • 4.Funding & fees. You need both accounts funded (USD on Kalshi, USDC on Polygon for Polymarket) — the screenshot shows Polymarket at $0.00. Kalshi fee ≈ 1-2¢/leg (already netted); Polymarket adds gas/withdrawal friction.
  • 5.Bucket boundaries. The middle buckets line up (86-87, 88-89, 90-91, 92-93); confirm identical integer-°F edges and the daily-high definition. The tails differ (Kalshi “85 or below / 94 or above” vs Polymarket “84-85 …”) — no clean arb there anyway.

5 Bottom line

The 88-89 and 90-91 pairs are genuinely fat spreads (~15-22¢ net per $1, ≈20-32%) because the two venues disagree on the modal bucket. Act on them only after confirming both markets settle on the same Miami daily-high reading for the same day. If the resolution sources differ, it isn’t arbitrage at all — it’s a directional bet on whose weather feed is right, with double-loss tail risk. 86-87 is thin; 92-93 disappears after fees.
